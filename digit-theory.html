Digit Theory: Modeling Money and Digital Identity as Dynamic Economic Variables

By MD. AL AMIN SOHAG · Published November 8, 2026 · Economic Systems Modeling

Conventional macroeconomic models frequently treat currency supply as an aggregate scalar and market participants as interchangeable, static nodes. In an era defined by programmable infrastructure and continuous cross-border verification, this separation no longer reflects operational reality.

1. Core Premise

Digit Theory establishes a mathematical framework in which monetary units and verified digital identity function as coupled, dynamic variables. Rather than viewing transaction velocity in isolation, the model evaluates how identity assurance directly alters systemic friction, trust latency, and capital allocation efficiency.

Foundational Axiom In a digitized economy, the utility and velocity of a unit of capital are proportional to the cryptographic and institutional verifiability of the identity layer through which it moves.

2. Implications for Industrial Resilience

When supply chains and enterprise ecosystems integrate verified identity layers directly into settlement architectures, counterparty risk drops measurably. Digit Theory provides quantitative parameters for stress-testing how localized identity or network disruptions propagate across broader economic grids.

3. Next Phase of Research

Ongoing modeling at SALSABILAH AMIN LIMITED focuses on integrating Digit Theory variables with the SOHAG Filter to simulate multi-node industrial settlement under high-entropy conditions.
